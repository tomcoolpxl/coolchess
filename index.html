How coolChess Works

Minimax & Advanced Pruning Algorithms

The AI uses Iterative Deepening with Principal Variation Search (PVS), Null-Move Pruning (NMP), Late Move Reductions (LMR), Reverse Futility Pruning (RFP), Quiescence Search with Delta Pruning, and a 64-bit Zobrist transposition table.

The algorithm works by:

  • Generating legal moves using fast piece-specific candidate rays, cached King coordinates, and in-place make/unmake transitions
  • Ordering moves via Transposition Table PV moves, promotions, MVV-LVA captures, Killer Moves, and History Heuristics
  • Applying Principal Variation Search (zero-window scouts), Null-Move Pruning, Late Move Reductions, and Reverse Futility Pruning to slash the branching factor
  • Recursively evaluating positions via Iterative Deepening up to Depth 7 and extending tactical captures via Quiescence Search
  • Caching evaluated positions in a 1M-entry 64-bit Zobrist transposition table with exact, upper, and lower bounds
  • Scoring positions with Tapered Piece-Square Tables (interpolating Middlegame and Endgame King tables), bishop pair bonuses, and piece mobility

7 difficulty levels control search depth and time budget: from Beginner (Depth 1) up to Expert (Depth 5), Master (Depth 6), and Grandmaster (Depth 7).

Opening Book

The first 2–3 moves use a pre-programmed opening book for instant, varied master openings:

  • White's first move: 4 options (1. e4, 1. d4, 1. c4 English, 1. Nf3 Réti)
  • Black's responses to 1. e4: e5, c5 (Sicilian), e6 (French), c6 (Caro-Kann)
  • Black's responses to 1. d4: d5, Nf6 (Indian), f5 (Dutch Defense)
  • Black's responses to 1. c4 & 1. Nf3: e5, c5, d5, Nf6 plus main-line 2nd-move continuations
